## Week 1 in one paragraph

An AI chatbot is a very good next-word guessing machine, built from billions of tuned
**parameters** and reading text in chunks called **tokens** (Day 1). "AI," "machine
learning," "deep learning," "generative AI," and "agentic AI" are nested, increasingly
specific terms, not interchangeable buzzwords — and plenty of "AI" is still a decade-old
prediction model wearing a new label (Day 2). What you type in — the **context window** — is
a notepad you hand the model fresh each time, separate from what it learned during training
(Day 3). There's now a genuine landscape of frontier and open-weight models, and comparing
them by name or parameter count alone tells you almost nothing about which one is right for
your task (Day 4). Some products can genuinely handle images, voice, or video; plenty of
"multimodal" claims are still demo-ware, not something you should run production work through
yet (Day 5). And some models now spend extra time "thinking" before answering, trading speed
for accuracy on harder problems — a real capability, not just a longer wait (Day 6).

That's the map. Today you use it to test the territory yourself.

## The exercise: same task, two or three tools, your own work

Pick **2–3 chatbot tools from different model families** — for example, something built on
Claude, something built on ChatGPT, and something built on Gemini. You don't need the latest
or most expensive tier of each; a mainstream consumer or business plan is fine. The point
isn't finding the single best product on the market. It's building the habit of checking
before trusting.

Then pick **2–3 real tasks from your actual job** this week — not toy examples. Good
candidates:

- A real email or message you genuinely need to send — a difficult one, not a one-liner.
- A real document, report, or set of meeting notes you need summarised or turned into
  next steps.
- A real question about a spreadsheet or dataset you already half-know the answer to, so
  you can tell if the AI gets it right.

**Run the exact same prompt through every tool.** This is the part people skip, and it's the
part that makes the comparison fair. If you word the request differently for each tool, you're
comparing your own prompting, not the tools. Write the prompt once, paste it unchanged into
each chatbot, and only then look at what comes back.

As you go, keep a simple running note — a notes app or a scrap document is fine — with one
line per tool per task: what it got right, what it got wrong, and how long it took you to
get a usable answer.

## What to look for while you compare

This is a light, practical taste of what Day 15 will cover properly under the name
**evals** — the formal discipline of measuring whether an AI system actually works. Today
you're just doing it by hand, on one task, for yourself. Four things worth checking on every
answer:

- **Accuracy against something you can verify.** If you already know the right number, name,
  or fact, check the answer against it directly — don't just judge whether it *sounds*
  right. A confident, well-written wrong answer is the most dangerous kind.
- **Speed versus doing it yourself.** Did the tool actually save time once you include the
  time spent checking and fixing its output — not just the time it took to generate?
- **Would you send this unchecked, or does it need a human pass first?** Be honest here.
  "Good first draft that needs editing" and "ready to send" are very different outcomes, and
  mixing them up is how bad AI output reaches a customer.
- **Where did the tools actually disagree?** Note any spot where one tool's answer
  meaningfully differed from another's on the exact same prompt. That gap is the most useful
  thing you'll learn all day — it's proof that "the AI said so" isn't one single voice.

Watch in particular for moments where a tool states something wrong with total confidence and
no hint of doubt — a **hallucination**. It's the single most important thing to catch today,
because it's also the easiest thing to miss if you're not specifically checking for it.

## What you should walk away with

Not a verdict on which tool is "best" — that answer changes by task, by week, and by which
company shipped an update most recently, so treat any confident "X is the best AI" claim you
hear elsewhere with the same skepticism you're practicing today. What you should actually
walk away with is a habit: **test a tool on a real, checkable task before you trust it with
something that matters**, and a first-hand sense that different tools genuinely do produce
different answers on the same real prompt — not just different marketing pages. That habit is
worth more over the next 23 days than any single tool ranking would have been.
