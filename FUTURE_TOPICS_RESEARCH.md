## Reviewed 2026-09-14

- **California builds a state-level AI-auditing infrastructure: independent verifiers, an auditor registry, and mandatory chatbot risk assessments.** On September 9–10, 2026, Governor Newsom signed a trio of bills that go beyond the EU-centric regulation framing Day 25 currently teaches. SB 813 creates a California Artificial Intelligence Standards and Safety Commission that will certify "Independent Verification Organizations" (IVOs) — outside experts who assess AI systems for compliance with state law — with certification starting by January 1, 2028. AB 1405 creates the nation's first state registry for AI auditors, with conflict-of-interest/independence standards and a 10-year records-retention rule, live by January 1, 2029. SB 1119 ("Adam's Law") requires chatbot operators to run child-safety risk assessments before launching or materially changing a companion chatbot (effective July 2027), plus recurring independent child-safety audits starting January 1, 2029, and opens a new legal pathway for families and the state to sue over violations. For a business leader, this is the first concrete sign that "AI auditing" is becoming a licensed profession with its own registry and standards body — not just an internal compliance checklist — and that running a customer-facing chatbot in California now carries a real audit and litigation timeline to plan around. McNerney — Legislature Approves SB 813](https://sd05.senate.ca.gov/news/legislature-approves-mcnerneys-landmark-bill-assess-artificial-intelligence-safety-risks), [Tech Times — California Signs First US AI Audit Law](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/327159/20260910/california-signs-first-us-ai-audit-law-frontier-labs-hiring-tools-now-scope.htm), [Hoodline — Newsom Signs Adam's Law](https://hoodline.com/2026/09/newsom-signs-adam-s-law-new-rules-force-ai-chatbots-to-screen-kids-for-self-harm/), [PYMNTS — California Starts Regulating the People Who Audit AI](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/california-starts-regulating-the-people-who-audit-ai/)

- **WebMCP: the browser itself is becoming an agent-connectivity standard.** Day 13 covers MCP and A2A, but WebMCP is a distinct, newer standard — a joint Google/Microsoft proposal now a Draft Community Group Report under the W3C's Web Machine Learning Community Group. It lets any website declare named, structured "tools" (e.g., "search inventory," "start checkout") that an in-browser AI agent can call directly, instead of the agent screen-scraping or parsing the page's DOM, with user authorization built into the design. Chrome shipped a flag-gated early preview in February 2026 and moved it to a broader origin trial by June 2026. For a business leader, this matters because it's a plausible path for "the browser as an agent platform" to become a cross-vendor baseline within 18–24 months — a consumer-facing website may soon need to expose a WebMCP tool surface the way it exposes a mobile-friendly layout today, which is a new build requirement, not just a new way for customers to shop. Source: [VentureBeat — Google, Microsoft unveil WebMCP for AI agents](https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/google-chrome-ships-webmcp-in-early-preview-turning-every-website-into-a), [Zuplo — What is WebMCP](https://zuplo.com/blog/what-is-webmcp), [AI Weekly — Google proposes WebMCP standard](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/google-proposes-webmcp-standard-for-browser-ai-agents)

- **Circular financing among AI infrastructure vendors is drawing real investor and regulatory scrutiny.** Not covered anywhere in Day 26/27's infrastructure and ROI material: reporting through 2026 has documented a tight loop of interlocking deals among chipmakers, cloud providers, and AI labs — Nvidia investing in and supplying OpenAI, OpenAI committing large sums to cloud providers like Oracle, those providers in turn buying more Nvidia chips — that some analysts estimate now runs into the hundreds of billions of dollars. Whether this reflects durable investment in real capacity, or (as skeptics who compare it to dot-com-era vendor-financing loops argue) revenue that's partly manufactured by the deals themselves, is genuinely unsettled and worth stating as an open question rather than a fact either way. What is a sourced, present-tense fact is that investors are now pushing AI vendors for "clean" revenue figures stripped of these arrangements — a real shift any business leader evaluating an AI infrastructure bet or vendor partnership should know about, independent of how the bubble debate resolves. Source: [Business Standard — Nvidia-OpenAI deal sparks concerns over circular financing](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/companies/news/nvidia-openai-deal-sparks-concerns-over-circular-financing-in-ai-boom-125092401589_1.html), [StartupHub.ai — The Circular Funding Deals Driving the AI Boom](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/startup-news/2026/the-circular-funding-deals-driving-the-ai-boom-and-the-looming-bubble-risk)

## Reviewed 2026-08-29

- **EU AI Act's "Digital Omnibus" reshuffles the August 2026 deadlines.** Day 25 mentions the EU AI Act only as "a real law, worth knowing exists" — the course doesn't reflect this update. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (published in the Official Journal July 24, 2026) delayed the high-risk obligations for stand-alone Annex III systems from August 2, 2026 to December 2, 2027. But the Article 50 transparency obligations — disclosing AI-generated content, labeling deepfakes, disclosing that a user is talking to a chatbot — were *not* delayed and took effect on schedule August 2, 2026. A business leader with an EU-facing chatbot or AI-generated content needs to know the transparency rules already apply even though the high-risk system rules got more runway. Source: [Jones Walker — "Yes, August 2 Still Matters"](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html), [Gibson Dunn — EU AI Act Omnibus Agreement](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)

- **The US is actively fighting to preempt state AI laws, not just leaving a patchwork.** The course's regulation coverage (Day 25) is EU-centric and doesn't mention this. Executive Order 14365 (signed December 11, 2025) launched a two-pronged federal strategy: an AI Litigation Task Force (est. January 9, 2026) suing to strike down state AI laws, plus a March 20, 2026 White House "National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence" urging Congress to pass a single federal standard that would preempt state rules. No federal statute has passed yet (as of this review), so the practical reality for US business leaders in the near term is still a state-by-state patchwork — but one under active legal and legislative attack, which is a real compliance-planning uncertainty worth knowing about. Source: [Ropes & Gray — White House Legislative Recommendations on Federal Preemption](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/the-white-house-legislative-recommendations-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-an), [Baker Botts — US AI Law Update, January 2026](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/january/us-ai-law-update)

- **Agent protocol governance consolidated, and a payments layer (AP2) has emerged on top of MCP/A2A.** Day 13 covers MCP and A2A as the connectivity standards, but two developments since then are new: (1) The Linux Foundation formed the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) in December 2025 to govern agentic-AI infrastructure neutrally; MCP was a founding project, and Axios reported August 17, 2026 that A2A is joining AAIF as well, with platinum members including AWS, Anthropic, Block, Bloomberg, Cloudflare, Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI — meaning the two standards the course teaches now sit under shared, vendor-neutral governance rather than being single-company projects. (2) Google's Agent Payments Protocol (AP2), an open standard sitting above MCP/A2A that lets AI agents make purchases on a user's behalf using cryptographically signed "mandates," has moved from announcement (September 2025) to a real spec (v0.2.0, April 2026) with 100+ endorsing organizations including Mastercard, PayPal, Coinbase, and American Express. OpenAI and Stripe separately built a competing Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP), first deployed inside ChatGPT's Instant Checkout in February 2026 — so there are now two rival agent-payment standards, not one. For a business leader, this is the first concrete sign that "agents that can actually transact" is becoming standardized infrastructure rather than a one-off vendor feature, and a governance/risk question (who's liable when an agent buys the wrong thing, and which standard does a vendor support?) worth flagging even before deciding whether to adopt it. Source: [Linux Foundation — AAIF announcement](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-the-formation-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation), [Google Cloud Blog — Announcing AP2](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol), [Cipher — Agentic Commerce Protocols Compared (MCP, A2A, ACP, AP2)](https://www.cipherprojects.com/blog/posts/agentic-commerce-protocols-mcp-a2a-acp-ap2-compared/)

## Reviewed 2026-08-24

- **World models as a distinct AI paradigm beyond LLMs** — Yann LeCun left Meta in late 2025 to found AMI Labs, raising roughly $1B+ (at a reported ~$3.5B valuation) specifically to build "world models" — systems trained to learn the rules of the physical world from real-time, multimodal sensory data rather than predicting the next token. Google DeepMind shipped Genie 3 (public access from late January 2026), a world model that generates explorable, real-time interactive environments, and Waymo adopted it for autonomous-driving simulation in February 2026. This matters to business leaders because it signals real money and top researchers betting that the next architectural shift won't just be "bigger LLMs" — it's a distinct category (spatial/physical reasoning, simulation, robotics) with a different cost and capability profile than the chatbot/RAG/agent stack the course currently teaches. Not currently covered by the course, which focuses on text/multimodal LLMs and agentic patterns.
  Sources: https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_(world_model) , https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/23/whos-behind-ami-labs-yann-lecuns-world-model-startup/

- **NIST's AI Agent Standards Initiative** — NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) launched a formal, three-pillar initiative on February 17, 2026, aimed at industry-led agent interoperability/security standards, community-led open-source agent protocol work (including the MCP ecosystem), and foundational agent identity/security research. A related NCCoE concept paper (also Feb 2026) proposes adapting existing identity/authorization frameworks specifically for AI agents acting autonomously on a user's behalf. This matters because it's the first dedicated U.S. government standards effort aimed squarely at agent security and identity — a concrete, citable government initiative for the "how do we govern agents that act on our behalf" question the course raises in Day 17 (security) and Day 25 (governance) but doesn't yet name.
  Source: https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/announcing-ai-agent-standards-initiative-interoperable-and-secure
